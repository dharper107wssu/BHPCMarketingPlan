Revenue + referral flywheel

First step: register on eMMA before anything else

Register BHC on Maryland's eMaryland Marketplace Advantage (eMMA) at emma.maryland.gov under NAICS codes 621112 and 621999. School districts, DJS, DHS, and courts use this portal to find and contract with providers. As an OON provider, your eMMA registration lets agencies issue purchase orders directly to BHC — bypassing insurance entirely. This takes 1–2 business days and costs nothing.

School system contracts

School systems are one of your best OON channels. IEE evaluations are paid directly by the district — no insurance involvement. For general contracted evaluations, many districts issue purchase orders to OON providers when their internal staff cannot meet IDEA's 60-day timeline. Click any card for the full strategy.

OON impact on schools: Mostly favorable. IEE evaluations are always direct district pay — your OON status is irrelevant. For contracted slots, lead with your expertise, turnaround time, and the district's legal obligation to complete evaluations within 60 days. Position OON as a feature: "We have no authorization requirements, no panel limitations, and no insurance delays."

Clinical provider referrals

Clinical providers remain your highest-volume source even OON. Your pitch shifts from insurance coverage to what families actually want: speed, expertise, and a comprehensive report that cuts through diagnostic ambiguity. Providers refer out-of-network constantly when their insurance-panel options have 6–18 month waits.

OON pitch to clinical providers: "We are a self-pay practice, which means we have no panel restrictions, no authorization delays, and no insurance red tape. We can typically complete an evaluation within 3–4 weeks of intake — compared to 6–18 months at panel-based hospital programs. Families who need answers now are exactly who we serve. We provide superbills so families can seek out-of-network reimbursement from their insurance if applicable."

90-day launch plan

Designed for parallel team execution. Each month has a primary focus but all prior outreach continues. OON/self-pay positioning is woven throughout. Click any block to expand the detailed task list.

M1
Month 1 — Foundation, Registration & First Wave (Days 1–30)
Infrastructure, eMMA, school systems, first clinical visits
›
  • Week 1: Register on eMMA (emma.maryland.gov) under NAICS 621112 and 621999. This is the single most important administrative step — it enables government purchase orders for school, DHS, DJS, and DDA referrals as an OON provider.
  • Week 1: Write letter of interest to BCPS Office of Special Education (6901 Charles St, Towson) and HCPS Office of Special Education (102 S. Hickory Ave, Bel Air). Reference IEE services specifically — districts pay directly regardless of insurance status.
  • Week 1: Identify and contact 3 private foster care / adoption agencies in Maryland (see Foster Care tab). Introduce BHC's evaluation services and request a meeting.
  • Week 2: Call DJS Baltimore Metro Regional Office and DJS Upper Chesapeake — ask for the Behavioral Health Coordinator. Mention eMMA registration and request vendor application materials.
  • Week 2: Call the juvenile division managing attorney at the Office of the Public Defender in Baltimore County (Towson) and Harford County (Bel Air). Attorney-funded evals are your primary juvenile channel as an OON provider.
  • Week 3: Begin visiting pediatric practices — 5 per week. Printed one-pager leads with turnaround time and superbill availability. Priority: Chesapeake Pediatrics, GBMC-affiliated practices, Luminis Health Harford County.
  • Week 3: Contact 3 VSO posts (VFW Bel Air, American Legion Aberdeen, DAV Baltimore County) — request to present at a post meeting about private DBQ evaluations.
  • Week 4: Design and print all channel-specific marketing materials. Each piece addresses OON status proactively. Set up HIPAA-compliant digital intake form (Jotform with BAA) and generate QR codes.
  • Week 4: Contact APG Family MWR or Employment Readiness office about TAP program speaker opportunity. Request info on their community resource provider process.
M2
Month 2 — Relationship Building & Digital Launch (Days 31–60)
Deepen connections, launch veteran ads, foster care and legal outreach
›
  • Week 5: Follow up every school and DJS contact from Month 1. Push for in-person meetings. Ask school district contacts specifically about IEE provider list inclusion.
  • Week 5: Launch targeted Facebook/Instagram ads for veteran DBQ evaluations. Target: veterans 25–60 within 35 miles of APG and Fort Meade. Ad copy leads with speed and VA rating expertise, not insurance.
  • Week 6: Schedule first lunch-and-learn at a pediatric or psychiatric practice. 20 minutes: eval types, how to refer, superbill process for families, turnaround time.
  • Week 6: Contact Maryland foster care and adoption agencies identified in Month 1. Send the foster care agency intro packet. Follow up by phone 5 days after delivery.
  • Week 6: Search va.gov/ogc/apps/accreditation for Maryland-based VA-accredited attorneys and claims agents. Send 10 targeted letters introducing BHC's DBQ evaluation services.
  • Week 7: Visit Juvenile Assessment Center (JAC) in Towson in person. Meet the clinical coordinator. Leave materials. Ask about evaluation referral process for youth needing deeper assessment.
  • Week 7: Contact 5 family law firms in Towson and Bel Air with your legal credential packet. Follow up by phone 5 days later. Emphasize court-ready report format and turnaround.
  • Week 8: Contact Maryland Bar Association family law and juvenile law sections about CLE presentation opportunities. One presentation reaches dozens of attorneys simultaneously.
  • Week 8: Contact Parents Place of Maryland and Maryland Disability Law Center — ask to be included on their IEE evaluator resource lists. These organizations field daily calls from families seeking independent evaluators.
  • Week 8: Send an email announcement about BHC's evaluation services to every LCPC in your BHP training and supervision network. This is your fastest-converting clinical referral activation.
M3
Month 3 — Contracts, Agreements & Scale (Days 61–90)
Close agreements, launch trainings, build referral tracking
›
  • Week 9: Follow up all school district contacts — push for a formal meeting with the Special Education Supervisor or Procurement office. Offer one IEE as a demonstration of quality and turnaround.
  • Week 9: Complete and submit DJS vendor application if received. Follow up with the Behavioral Health Coordinator by phone.
  • Week 10: Schedule and deliver first BHP training or CE workshop — lead with the Trauma-Informed Assessment workshop to DHS or foster care agency staff. This simultaneously generates training revenue and builds the foster care referral relationship.
  • Week 10: Present at first VSO post meeting. Bring veteran flyers, DBQ service menu, and QR booking code. Offer to answer questions about the VA disability claim process after your presentation.
  • Week 11: Formalize written referral agreements with any family law, disability, or immigration attorneys who have expressed interest. Simple one-page agreement: turnaround, format, fee, payment terms.
  • Week 11: Review Facebook/Google ad performance — shift budget to highest-converting audiences. Consider adding a Google search ad campaign for "private DBQ evaluation Maryland" and "psychological evaluation Baltimore."
  • Week 12: Team debrief — which channels produced the first contacts or referrals? Which outreach did not generate response? Prioritize follow-up on warm contacts, redirect effort away from cold ones.
  • Week 12: Build a referral tracking spreadsheet: source, date referred, eval type, payment method, status, revenue. This data tells you where to double down in Quarter 2.
  • Week 12: Contact CASA de Maryland and immigration law firms in Baltimore. Send introduction to BHC's forensic psychological evaluation capacity for asylum, VAWA, and hardship cases.
